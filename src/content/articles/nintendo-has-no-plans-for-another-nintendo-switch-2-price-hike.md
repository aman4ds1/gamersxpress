## What happened

Nintendo does not plan to raise the Switch 2's price again and is not weighing any further increase for now. Nintendo's Shuntaro Furukawa said the wider memory situation has barely changed, and that the company has locked in enough memory to keep building consoles at today's prices.

Nintendo has not said how much memory it secured, and Furukawa did not say how long those supply agreements last. The company raised Switch 2 prices earlier this year because memory costs were climbing, announcing a $50 increase in May. The new prices took effect on September 1, 2026 in the US, Canada, Japan and Europe, and the UK followed toward the end of summer.

## Why gamers should care

Nintendo says it has enough memory to build more Switch 2 consoles and keep up with demand, and it has no plans for a further increase for now. The earlier rises traced back to memory costs, which AI-driven shortages of memory and storage have pushed up worldwide.

### Pricing

- **US:** now $499.99, up from $449.99 (September 1, 2026)
- **Japan:** now ¥59,980, up from ¥49,980
- **Europe (My Nintendo Store):** now €499.99, up from €469.99

## Context and comparison

In February 2026 Nintendo said no price rise was imminent despite rising RAM costs. The squeeze has also reached its supply chain: one of its longtime ROM suppliers, whose memory goes into Switch cartridges, left the business to chase more profitable work. Nintendo has also raised US prices on Switch 2 accessories because of tariffs.

Switch 2 shipments came to 2.67 million units from July to September, down 41% from a year earlier, bringing the total to 26.35 million. Hardware sales have fallen for several quarters compared with the 2025 launch period, and Nintendo expects to sell 16.5 million Switch 2 consoles in the fiscal year ending March 2027.

Rivals face the same pressure. Sony raised PS5 and PS5 Pro prices several times, and says it has already agreed memory volumes with suppliers for its next fiscal year. Xbox has raised Series X and Series S prices in multiple rounds, most recently in August 2026. NVIDIA's GeForce RTX 50-series is reportedly set for price increases of up to $500.
